# Batch — the slot door

The front door for the batch workflow: **what runs unattended**, composed as a
shift brief the watcher runs. Drive it from a chat; nothing here needs a terminal.

Read [`../../agents/conductors/batch/AGENTS.md`](../../agents/conductors/batch/AGENTS.md)
before acting — it holds the constraints, why each exists, and the full slot
procedure. This page is how to *converse* with it.

## The three things a human says

**1. "What should I run?"** — `/batch`, or any ask for the next batch.

Run `bin/pyauto-brain batch plan` and return the **BatchDecision as written**.
Do not re-rank it. Do not quietly add a member it rejected. Two parts must
survive into your reply because they are the point of the whole surface:

- the **review-minute total** — the budget is the human's hour, not a task count;
- the **other lane's ready count**, when this session cannot plan it: *"4
  laptop-lane tasks are ready (3 ral, 1 local-dev) — run this from the
  laptop."*

An empty batch is a **finding**: at the backpressure cap nothing costs zero
review-min; with an empty `queue.md` an unattended plan dips no backlog. Say which.

**2. "Here are the ones I want."** — a paste of paths from the Mind dashboard,
or a description ("something on the numba path", "the next euclid slice").

Resolve it to prompt paths; plan exactly that set as a brief's named tasks, or
`batch plan --attended` (else an empty `queue.md` stops it). **Under a shift brief**
(`AUTONOMY.md`, "Shift briefs and unattended `glance`") the brief *is* the human's composition:
its named tasks go first, then the backlog dip its policy allows (`none` |
`fill-idle` | `scoped:` plus `exclude:`). Backlog the brief does not cover is
never dipped, and every dipped pick carries a one-line reason in the digest.
`brief --check` says why any named task will not start (AUTONOMY.md, R5 and R2); a queued epic ledger queues its members (#696), and plan, check and digest say per epic why it is idle.
Report, per task they named:

- **taken** — with its tier and review-minutes;
- **rejected, and why** — in the planner's own words. Most common:
  `autonomy human-required — would park at ship` (it cannot run unattended; it
  would come back as a question — `supervised` no longer parks, AUTONOMY.md
  "Decide-and-flag"), `Status: says the work is already done`, `epic <slug>
  phase N is not next`, `<repo> held by <task> while its worker runs` (its
  PRs then merge in PR order, M3.6j), `would exceed the budget`.

If everything they named is rejected, **say so plainly and offer the planner's
own proposal instead** — never hand back a batch they did not ask for as though
it were what they wanted.

The human may overrule any rejection except two: a `Blocked-by:` that is still
open, and `Unattended: never`. Those are not preferences.

**3. "Go."** — the dispatch. This is the launch, and it is the human's act
(`AUTONOMY.md`, "What a batch launch is"): membership fixed at approval, the
grant expiring with the shift — the interval from dispatch to the `review-at:`
they declare here. Never dispatch on your own initiative, on a schedule, or
because a batch looks ready. **Writing a shift brief with `review-at:` is the
dispatch** (2026-11-03): inside that brief's scope, and only until its
`review-at:`, the watcher may start sessions without a second "go"
(`PyAutoMind/policy/shift_watcher.md`).

In order:

1. Consult vitals (`bin/pyauto-brain vitals`). If Heart is YELLOW, **ask the
   human to acknowledge the reason set for this shift** and record it verbatim.
   You may not acknowledge it for them, and a set carried from an earlier batch
   is void. **Write `expected-effects:` lines with them** (2026-08-31): one line
   per member with reach, naming what it may legitimately change in the
   organism's health ("member X merges into PyAutoArray and may stale release
   validation") — ask the human for these whenever a member merges into a
   library; they are the ONLY thing a later "generated by an earlier member"
   YELLOW classification may match against (`AUTONOMY.md`, leg 4).
2. Write the shift brief `PyAutoMind/batches/<YYYY-MM-DD>-<am|pm>.md` —
   `brief --new … --out` composes it and makes its shift issue; `brief --check`
   validates it; pushing it is the launch: [`reference.md`](reference.md)
   "Launching a shift brief". `review-at:` is when the human expects to be back
   and where the grant expires: **if they did not say, ask before
   dispatching** — never infer it from a schedule, a previous shift, or the
   time of day.
3. Confirm what was launched, and stop. Do not follow the runs: the watcher
   starts the workers, and each **ends at its deliverable**
   (`PyAutoMind/policy/end_at_deliverable.md`).

**Brief verbs** (`batch brief --check`, `batch plan --brief`, `batch digest`):
[`reference.md`](reference.md). Run the check before pushing a brief; it refuses a live brief without `issue:` or with an expired CLI login. `brief --new` also runs the keep-Heart-green lane: a `## Heart` section and up to 3 bug drafts for fixable Heart reasons (only with `--out` and a non-shared `--mind`; named as tasks only with `--green pick`); it never releases (`batch green` = dry run; `agents/conductors/batch/AGENTS.md`).

## The watcher (phases 3a–3d)

One deterministic tick per timer firing; the timer is installed by the human, never a session.
After `review-at:` (or an early close, once nothing is left to do) its one remaining act is the digest: written into the record
and posted once on the brief's `issue:`. A `shadow: yes` brief plans and digests
but spawns nothing — the pilot's baseline; every shift keeps one line in `PyAutoMind/batches/pilot/pilot.jsonl`, rendered
to `pilot.md` (`?` cells the human's at check-in). A parked worker's question is filed in the questions inbox (Mind `questions.md`, cleared with `/questions`) and the digest says how many, with no @mention mid-shift. `batch ready` (the readiness pass) runs at shift end and on request: it files each gate on the queue's next units as a `ruling` in the inbox, and the digest ends "The next shift needs N rulings from you" (dry run by default; `--queue <slot>`, `--apply` on a session branch). Once a worker's `glance` PR is green, the
watcher starts one independent close-out session that merges it or refuses; each merge adds a row to the stage-2 table in `PyAutoMind/autonomy_log.md`.
What a tick does, its state, caps and the install commands: `agents/conductors/batch/AGENTS.md` "The watcher".
**`/batch watch`** ("how is the shift going?"): run `bin/pyauto-brain batch watch --record <brief> --once` and relay it as written; with no slot on this machine, read the status comment on its `issue:` ([`reference.md`](reference.md)). The human steers with their own comment there (`/pause`, `/resume`, `/drop <task>`, `/stop`, `/add <prompt>`, `/cap merges|workers|sessions|tokens <n>`, `/kill <task>`); never post one for them. `batch watch --stream --record <brief>` prints the shift's event stream (`events.jsonl`, local reads only) until it ends — the cockpit's watch ([`reference.md`](reference.md)).

## When they come back — the digest, review, close

**The digest is the review surface** (2026-11-03): the watcher writes it into
the record and posts it once on the shift's `issue:`; see
[`reference.md`](reference.md). Order: failures first, then anything labelled
`decision-taken`, then clean.

**Before reading any PR from a shift, the independent adversary leg must have
run** (`AUTONOMY.md` leg 5): `bin/pyauto-brain review --task <name> --witness
"<the prompt's Witness:>" --adversary`, **in a fresh Opus session or sub-agent
that did not write the branch** (Fable only when the human asks). A self-run adversary leg is an absent
leg, not a weak one, and recording it as run is a false ledger row. If it has
not run, say so rather than reviewing as though it had.

The human rules; enacting is yours, member by member:

- **merge** — `/prm <PR>`; for a science member, write the ruling into the
  project's own ledger (DECISIONS, harvest table, results summary) verbatim.
- **tweak** — their one line becomes a follow-up prompt *you* draft, placed at
  the **top of `queue.md`**. They never write a prompt file.
- **reject** — route it to `condemned.md`.

**Review closes the batch; follow-ups never block the next one** (2026-08-31):
they are queued for the next batch, never executed inside the review. What
"complete" means, and asking for `review-minutes-actual:`: [`reference.md`](reference.md).

**`delivered:` is not "green".** A cloud session's green status means it exited
without an infrastructure error — not that the task succeeded. A member counts
as delivered only with a PR that has a non-empty diff and checks that ran green.
A member that ended green with no PR is reported **not delivered**, loudly, at
the top.

## Never

- Dispatch without an explicit go from the human in this conversation, or an
  open shift brief whose scope covers the task.
- Acknowledge a Heart YELLOW or RED on their behalf, or reuse a previous
  shift's reason set.
- Add a member the planner rejected without saying which rejection you are
  overruling and why.
- Present the proposal as a schedule. The schedule may carry the timing; it
  never carries the authority.
- Dispatch without a `review-at:` in the batch record, or invent one for them.
- Execute a review follow-up inside the review, or hold the next batch waiting
  on one — follow-ups go to the top of `queue.md` and run in the next batch.
